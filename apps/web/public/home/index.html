More than a bank-statement importer

The hard parts of the books, done properly.

Plenty of software reads a bank statement. NZOSA keeps a real set of books, and does the work an accountant usually charges for.

Coding that learns from you

Rules are written from how you or your accountant coded before. You never type keywords, and a correction sticks.

GST, reconciled

Returns built from your coding, set beside what you filed with IRD, with the lines behind every difference.

IR10 and IR3

The IR10 box by box as it is filed, and each owner's IR3 with their rental share, other income and provisional tax.

Rentals, the way accountants set them out

Each property its own entity and owners, rental schedules with last year beside them, and property manager statements.

Payroll and leave

PAYE, KiwiSaver and student loan, employment information for myIR, and leave owed under the Holidays Act.

AI where it helps

Optional, with your own Claude, ChatGPT or Gemini key: suggestions for lines it has never seen, and property manager statements read for you.

Matching that understands money

Transfers between your own accounts, invoices settled by part payments, and Stripe payouts split into sale, surcharge and fee.

Assets and vehicles

A fixed asset register with depreciation and disposals, and vehicle trips claimed at IRD's kilometre rates.

Books that have to balance

Every line lands in a double-entry ledger, so a mistake shows as a figure that does not add up, not a report that quietly reads wrong.

Bank feeds

Connect your bank. The lines come to you.

Link your New Zealand bank accounts through Akahu, and Wise through its own connection, and new transactions arrive ready to code. Prefer files? Statement exports from your bank work too, and the same line is never counted twice.

Your bank login and tokens stay with you: on your own computer they never leave the books' folder.

Akahu bank feedsThe main New Zealand banks, through your own Akahu connection.

WiseMulti-currency balances, with the amount billed in your account's currency.

Statement filesBank and card exports, loans included. Duplicates are recognised.

Coding

Most of a year codes itself. You deal with the handful that matter.

Load the coding from Xero or your spreadsheet and NZOSA lines up every suggestion against what was actually done, then writes the rules from the agreement.

  • Accept the bulk in a few clicks; disagreements are set out on their own.
  • It asks rather than guesses: a line it cannot place stays in front of you.
  • Entertainment split 50/50 with the GST right, in one click.
GST

Every GST return, beside what you actually filed.

Load myIR's GST return summary and each period is shown against what the books now say. Click a period to see exactly which lines make the difference, and write the reason against it.

  • Payments, invoice or hybrid basis; two-monthly or six-monthly.
  • Transfers never reach a return, and refunds go back to the right box.
  • Each owner of a shared rental can file for their own share.
Year end

Rental schedules, IR3s and IR10, ready for your accountant.

Each property's year on one card: its schedule, the property manager's statements and trips made to it. The owners' IR3s take their share automatically.

  • Property manager statements entered by hand or read with AI.
  • IR10 every box from 1 to 60, in whole dollars, as filed.
  • Validated against a practitioner's signed accounts, to the dollar.
Private & open

Your books are yours.

On your own computer

Your books are plain files in a folder you can back up and read without this software. Nothing is uploaded, and the app makes no network requests of its own.

Open source

Free software under the GNU AGPL. Read every line on GitHub, check how a figure is worked out, or host your own copy online.

Moving from Xero

Bring your Xero files and NZOSA lays its figures beside Xero's, so you can see they agree before you rely on them.

Try it on invented books first.

The demo is a coffee roastery and two rentals, part way through a year. Change anything; nothing is real.
