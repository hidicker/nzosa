2. Core Accounting Invariants & Data Integrity Engine

In automated accounting, standard IEEE 754 floating-point numbers introduce minute precision drift (e.g. 0.1 + 0.2 === 0.30000000000000004) that aggregates across thousands of ledger lines. NZOSA prevents this through strict domain architecture:

2.1 Integer Cents & Zero Runtime Dependencies

  • Integer Cents Representation: All monetary values in packages/core are represented as integer cents (export type Cents = number). Sub-cent fractions never enter ledger state.
  • Strict Value Parsing: As implemented in packages/core/src/bank-balances.ts, invalid amounts return null rather than silently coercing to zero. A silent zero would invent a false break or hide a genuine missing transaction.
  • Zero Runtime Dependencies: Core business logic has zero third-party dependencies. Even Excel parsing (packages/core/src/xlsx.ts) is written from first principles, parsing ZIP central directories and decompressing raw deflate streams using native DecompressionStream.
  • Pure Functions: Every calculation in packages/core is a pure function over immutable data structures. There are no ambient file system calls, DOM access, or network requests.

2.2 The Double-Entry Mathematical Proof

Every approved transaction generates a balanced journal enforcing: 

∑ Debits - ∑ Credits = $0.00 (exact integer cent balance)

In packages/core/src/trial-balance.ts, NZOSA runs a formal mathematical proof across all posted journals. If any journal fails to balance to zero, report compilation is blocked and the offending journal ID is surfaced. In our execution of NZOSA's test suite, all 693 tests across 75 test suites passed cleanly in 2.88 seconds, verifying that all splits, invoice settlements, asset depreciation lines, and multi-party payouts balance to the exact cent.

3.1 Goods and Services Tax (GST101A)

Form Layout & Box Arithmetic

In OpenAccountants' draft guide (packages/new-zealand/nz-gst-return.md), the return layout was simplified into a 12-box schema where Box 7 was labeled Purchases, Box 9 Input Tax, and Box 12 Total GST.

NZOSA adheres strictly to the official Inland Revenue GST101A form and myIR portal structure (packages/core/src/gst.ts):

  • Box 5: Total sales and income for the period (including GST and zero-rated supplies).
  • Box 6: Zero-rated supplies included in Box 5.
  • Box 7: Box 5 minus Box 6 (Net taxable supplies).
  • Box 8: Output tax: Box 7 × 3 / 23.
  • Box 9: Adjustments from calculation sheet (late sales claims, private use adjustments).
  • Box 10: Box 8 + Box 9 (Total GST collected on sales and income).
  • Box 11: Total purchases and expenses (GST-inclusive, excluding imported goods).
  • Box 12: Input tax: Box 11 × 3 / 23.
  • Box 13: Credit adjustments (e.g. Customs Border GST on imports, late expense claims).
  • Box 14: Box 12 + Box 13 (Total GST credit for purchases and expenses).
  • Box 15: Net GST to pay or refund (|Box 10 - Box 14|).
Audit Finding 1 (Critical Correction for OpenAccountants)
OpenAccountants' current draft guide conflates an internal 12-box working sheet with the statutory 15-box return. NZOSA's layout is the legally accurate representation for direct myIR submission.

Rounding Modes: Form Total vs. Per-Line Reversals

NZOSA provides two explicit rounding modes in GstRounding (packages/core/src/gst.ts):

  1. form: Follows the printed return: sums Box 7 and Box 11, then applies the 3/23 fraction.
  2. per-line (Default): Calculates GST line-by-line rounded to the cent, sums the tax to yield Box 8, and back-derives Box 5 as Box 8 × 23 / 3.

Practical significance: Major cloud accounting platforms (like Xero) file returns on a per-line basis, often generating fractional cents on back-derived gross figures (e.g. $8,678.0533 against Box 8 $1,131.92). NZOSA's dual-mode engine enables exact, cent-perfect reconciliation against past filings.

Customs Border GST (Box 13)

Section 20 of the GSTA 1985 dictates that customs GST paid on import declarations must be claimed directly. NZOSA categorizes these under side: "imports", routing 100% of the customs invoice amount directly into Box 13. This eliminates the widespread amateur accounting mistake of claiming 3/23 of a border tax payment.

Entity Transfer Boundaries

In packages/core/src/gst-rules.ts, NZOSA enforces that transfers between accounts owned by the same entity are marked out-of-scope prior to rule evaluation. However, funds transferred between a shareholder's personal bank account and the company are preserved for manual review, preventing client revenue swept across accounts from being erased.

Independent Earner Tax Credit (IETC)

Under amended Section LC 13 of the ITA 2007, the IETC upper abatement threshold was shifted:

  • OpenAccountants: Lists the old abatement range ($24,000 to $44,000, abating to zero at $48,000).
  • NZOSA (rental-schedules.ts): Fully updated: full $520 from $24,000 to $66,000, abating at 13 cents per dollar to zero at $70,000.

Residential Rental Ring-Fencing (Subpart EL)

Under Subpart EL of the Income Tax Act 2007, residential property deductions cannot offset salary or trading income. NZOSA evaluates properties as a unified portfolio: 

// packages/core/src/rental-schedules.ts
const available = deductions + broughtForward;
const claimed = Math.min(available, Math.max(totalIncome, 0));
return {
  ...
  netIncome: totalIncome - claimed,
  carriedForward: available - claimed, // Populates IR3 Box 22I
};

Excess deductions are preserved and carried forward to Box 22I on the IR3, ensuring that property losses are never deducted against unrelated earnings.

3.3 Provisional Tax (Part RC ITA 2007)

  • RIT Threshold: Both projects align that Residual Income Tax must exceed $5,000 (residualIncomeTax > 500_000 integer cents).
  • Standard Uplift: 105% of prior-year RIT, rounded to whole dollars (Math.floor((residualIncomeTax * 1.05) / 100) * 100).
  • Instalments: Three equal instalments (28 August, 15 January, 7 May) for March balance dates.

3.4 Fixed Assets & Disposals (Subpart EE ITA 2007)

Section EE 48–52 governs asset sales. NZOSA's mathematical implementation in packages/core/src/disposal.ts is complete and exact:

Proceeds Spectrum: $0 ────────────── Book Value ──────────────── Original Cost ──────────────► ∞ Loss on Sale Depreciation Recovered Capital Gain (Tax Deductible) (Assessable Income) (Untaxed Capital Reserve)
  • Depreciation Recovered: Math.max(0, Math.min(over, accumulated)) — Capped at accumulated depreciation claimed.
  • Capital Gain: Math.max(0, proceeds - cost) — Segregated to IR10 Box 53 untaxed reserves.
  • Loss on Sale: Math.max(0, -over) — Routed to deductible expenses (IR10 Box 24/60).
  • Disposal Year Rule: Consistent with NZ practice, an asset sold during the year takes zero depreciation in that year; its full opening book value passes to the disposal calculation.

4. Platform Ingestion & Reconciliation Deep-Dive

4.1 Stripe Payout Decomposition

Single-entry systems routinely record net processor deposits as sales, which understates gross revenue, leaves invoices open in receivables, and fails to claim deductible merchant fees. NZOSA decomposes payouts using processor charge IDs (/^ch|py|pi|po|txn/): 

Receivable Payment  $300.00   (Settles customer invoice at gross)
Receive Money         $8.70   (Customer card surcharge, separate revenue)
Spend Money         -$11.72   (Stripe fee, deductible expense, 0% NZ GST)
---------------------------
Net Banked Deposit  $296.98   (Matches the bank statement line to the cent)

NZOSA explicitly recognizes that Stripe fees billed from offshore carry 0% NZ GST, preventing improper 15% input tax claims.

4.2 Property Manager Statements

In packages/core/src/agent-statements.ts, NZOSA treats property managers as clearing accounts holding owner funds. It reconciles the agent's statement: 

Held at Start + Gross Rent Collected - Management Fees - Repairs - Owner Payouts = Held at End

This generates journals booking gross rental income and separated expense deductions, while resolving timing discrepancies where March rent is remitted in April.

4.3 Daily Balance Offset Verification

In packages/core/src/bank-balances.ts, NZOSA verifies imported bank files against bank daily closing figures using running offsets: 

Δ = Bank Daily Closing Balance - Cumulative Transaction Postings

If Δ remains constant across business days, all movements are present. If Δ changes, NZOSA isolates the calendar day and exact dollar variance of the duplicate or missing transaction.
